# Round 1: resolve red flags instead of caveating them

**Status:** run 12 Oct 2026. **Reverted**: no measurable effect.

## Behaviour targeted

Haiku notices a red flag in its own work and commits to its first answer anyway, mentioning the flag as a caveat instead of fixing it. The red flags it notices and skips past:

- its result matches no option;
- a check it ran itself points against its pick;
- a given quantity or condition goes unused;
- it spots a flaw in an option and dismisses it;
- it relies on recall or "typical practice" when the question gives enough to derive the answer.

## Evidence (train split only)

- Of the 45 failing train answers, 21 answers across 10 questions show the behaviour in the visible output. At most that's 21/153 = 13.7 points of train accuracy.
- Hedging language ("recall", "did not verify", "judgement", "assume", "closest") is a strong signal. 26 of the 43 answers containing it are wrong, against 19 of the 110 answers without it.
- In two contrasting cases, the same question was answered correctly on another try when Haiku followed its own check through to the end.

## Change

v0 is kept unchanged, with a new paragraph appended. It lists the five red flags. It says a red flag means the work is unfinished, not that the question is flawed, and gives a resolution order: recheck setup, derive from the given data, re-test dismissed options. It forbids keeping the first pick with a caveat. As a last resort, the model picks the answer whose remaining gap traces to one systematic difference.

**Review edit:** in that last sentence I dropped the analyzer's example "a slip in the question's numbers", which invites blaming the question. That contradicts the rule two sentences earlier.

## Expected effect and risks

- **Expected gain:** about +3 to +7 points on train, mostly HLE and GPQA. The low end is near the ±3.1-point noise floor.
- **Regression risk:** correct "judgement call" answers might get reworked into wrong ones.
- **Cost risk:** more output tokens on hard questions.
- **Flagged answer keys:** the analyzer thinks three HLE references (`hle-671ee9…`, `hle-6710b…`, `hle-67325a…`) disagree with a fuller derivation. That's worth a later look as possible faulty keys. It's not a reason to change the prompt.

## Result

| | v0 (baseline) | v1 | Change (paired 95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Test** (99 questions) | 68.0% | 68.0% | −0.3 pts [−2.4, +2.0], not significant |
| Train (51 questions) | 70.6% | 68.6% | −2.0 pts [−6.6, +2.0], not significant |
| Cost (Haiku + judge) | $0.80 | $0.83 | |
| Mean output tokens | 1,941 | 1,981 | +2% |

**Did the change engage?** Barely. On train:

- Answers with unresolved-caveat language went from 10 to 12 of 153, so the behaviour it targeted didn't go away.
- Visible answers got about 10% longer, and total output (including hidden thinking) about 6% longer.
- The 10 targeted questions improved from 4/30 to 7/30 correct, but losses elsewhere on train (mostly QASPER) cancelled that out.

**Conclusion:** the instruction nudged a few targeted cases but didn't change the habit, and it didn't generalise to the held-out questions. v0 stays the best prompt.
